Design: the proposal

Design: the proposal

A draft for Josu, written on 17 October 2026 from the brief and the KaleidaGraph inventory (L006), revised the same day for the practices (L011) and for the app as a fork of the TRAE IDE (L009). Every question at the end now has a logged decision (L010, L013); the options weighed are kept under each.

The shape: core, app, ecosystem

As in diff-ref-struct and difes: the core holds every calculation (the table, the formulas, the units and uncertainties, the fits, the plots' geometry), has no user interface, a test for every feature and a command-line path to every feature. The app is a Mac window around it and holds no calculation: it turns numbers into pixels and text (APP_CONVENTIONS.md §0). The ecosystem is this site, its data and its tools. One repository holds the three (L003).

The app: a fork of the TRAE IDE

decided (L009, confirmed L013) The app is coded as a fork of the TRAE IDE, not on diff-ref-struct's app (R10). TRAE is built on Code – OSS, the open source of VS Code, whose source is public under the MIT licence; TRAE's own is not. So the fork is of Code – OSS, with TRAE's look. What that means:

  • The window is VS Code's workbench by construction: the activity bar, the side bar with its views, the editor area with its groups and splits, the panel, the secondary side bar (our inspector), the status bar, the command palette, settings, keybindings, themes, localisation, window state restored. Everything §1–§3, §6 and §11 of APP_CONVENTIONS.md ask for is there before a line of ours is written; what remains to write is what goes in it.
  • The modules are built-in contributions, written as VS Code extensions are (views, custom editors, commands, settings, menus, a language for the formula console), bundled with the app and not installable separately at first. The family's extension idea (diff-ref-struct's I02) is then VS Code's own extension model.
  • The core is reached from Node: the workbench's extension host calls it as a native module, a process over stdio, or WebAssembly; which is part of Q1. The rule stands: no calculation in the window.
  • What is removed from Code – OSS: the text-editing workbench stays (the Report module edits text, the formula console is an editor), but the language services, the debugger, the terminal, the SCM views and the marketplace are hidden or stripped, so that a student opens a laboratory tool, not an editor with a plot in it.
  • What is taken from diff-ref-struct's app as notes: its module interface, the files-by-role sidebar, the shared plot component's behaviour, the nophysics test, the forty-one requests of its stream and their answers, the state-restoration checks: carried over as requirements and tests, written anew in the workbench's terms.
  • The cost: a Code – OSS checkout is large and its first build takes hours; the fork follows upstream at its own pace (a merge every few months); the app weighs what VS Code weighs. The cost was weighed against Tauri with dockview, which gave diff-ref-struct its window at a tenth of the weight, and set aside by Josu for the window itself.

The window: TRAE's, holding KaleidaGraph's things

The brief asks for the interface of the TRAE IDE (R02). APP_CONVENTIONS.md, carried over from diff-ref-struct, already describes that window in full: the activity bar, the sidebar, the main area of tabs that split, the bottom panel, the inspector, the status bar, every separator draggable, every panel collapsible and movable, native window tabbing, ⌘K. What is this project's is what goes in each place:

  • The sidebar shows the project's files by their role (Data, Plots, Fits, Layouts, Report, Versions, the Bench's session), as diff-ref-struct's W5 does, never the folder alphabetically; hovering a file says what it holds (columns, points, the fit's \(\chi^2\)).
  • The main area holds tables, plots, fit reports and the report's pages as tabs; a table and its plot side by side is the normal arrangement, and the Bench is one tab that can go full screen.
  • The bottom panel is Formula Entry made a console: a formula typed runs on the project; its history scrolls; the fit log and the problems (a unit mismatch, a fit that did not converge, a reading out of range) have their tabs.
  • The inspector shows what is selected: a column's name, unit, uncertainty and format; a plot's axes, variables, markers, error bars; a fit's model, parameters with uncertainties, \(\chi^2\)/ν, the residuals. KaleidaGraph's Axis Options, Variable Settings and Plot Options dialogs become this one panel.
  • ⌘K finds every command, every file of the project, every column and every manual page; the Gallery's 29 plot types and the fits are commands there as well as menus.

The modules

Six modules over one project folder, each with its icon, its menu, its commands and its manual page (APP_CONVENTIONS.md §11); the first four are KaleidaGraph's four windows made modules, the fifth is the report, the sixth is the brief's live mode.

Module What it holds From KaleidaGraph New here
Data Tables of named, typed columns; entry, import, sort, mask, series, column statistics. The data window, the Data and Functions menus. A unit and an uncertainty on every column (I03); named columns; the table as a node of the pipeline (I04).
Plots The Gallery's plot types; axes, ticks, legends, variables, error bars; the toolbox's selection and identify tools; export. The plot window, the Gallery and Plot menus, the toolbox. A plot always follows its data: no Update Plot. The residuals under a fitted plot. Axes labelled from the columns' names and units.
Fits The nine standard fits and the general nonlinear fit with a library of equations; weighting, limits, derivatives; parameters with uncertainties, \(\chi^2\)/ν, R\(^{2}\), confidence bands; residuals. The Curve Fit menu and the General Curve Fit Definition dialog. A fit is a file of the project, refit whenever its data change; its report is what the lab report needs (I11).
Formulas Formula Entry as a console with named columns; the library of functions and fit definitions; macros and scripts. Formula Entry, the Library, the Macros menu, plot and formula scripts. Units and uncertainties propagated (I03); KaleidaGraph's notation accepted and its text files imported (I10).
Report Layouts of plots; figures and tables exported by the family's rules; the report skeleton. The layout window, export of images. Figures typeset by LaTeX, identical geometry across a series (I05); everything regenerated from the project.
Bench The live mode: a prepared table, the next cell, the plots and fits redrawn on each reading, the session recorded. Nothing: KaleidaGraph has no live mode. I01, with I02, I07, I08 around it.

The Bench, in one session

The student opens the practice's file (prepared by the teacher, I02) and presses Bench. The window folds to one table and its plots. The next cell is large, named with its column and unit ("t, s"). The student reads the chronometer, types 2,34, Return: the reading is in the table, the point is on the plot, the fit's line has moved and its slope's uncertainty has narrowed, all within the keystroke; the reading's row shows a tick, or an orange mark if it lies far from the fit, which is a suggestion to remeasure, not a refusal. Tab moves to the next column; one key repeats the last reading; one key undoes it. No dialog opens. The session file records every commit with its time. When the series is complete, the Bench is left and the project is the ordinary one: the table, the plots, the fits, ready for the report. Every number shown was computed by the core, which the app asked on each commit.

The data model

  • A column has a name, a type (number, integer, date, time, text, as KaleidaGraph's), a unit, an uncertainty rule (none; a constant, the instrument's resolution; a column of its own; a percentage), a format, and either cells or a formula.
  • A formula names columns and library functions; its units are checked and its uncertainties propagated; KaleidaGraph's c0, m0, cell(), index() stay valid.
  • A fit names a plot's variables (or columns), a model (standard or a user equation), weights, limits and starting values, and holds its last solution, parameters, covariance, \(\chi^2\)/ν and residuals.
  • A plot names its variables and holds its look; a layout names plots; the report names layouts and fits.
  • The graph of what depends on what is the core's (I04): a change recomputes downstream, in order, once.

The project's files

A project is a folder (R20 of diff-ref-struct, carried over): Data/ the tables (the app's text format, and the imported originals untouched), Plots/, Fits/, Layouts/, Report/, Versions/ (a version before every Bench session and on demand), Bench/ the session records, and the practice's template at the top. Every file is readable text (the data as CSV with a header of units and uncertainties; the rest as a readable format of ours), so that a student can open any of it without the app, and a teacher can diff two groups' projects. A file dictionary like diff-ref-struct's (R27 there) will fix the names when the core writes them.

For Josu to decide

Q1 · The language of the core, and how the workbench reaches it

  • Rust, as diff-ref-struct's core: one binary for the command line, fast enough for a fit per keystroke without thought; diff-ref-struct's least-squares engine (Levenberg–Marquardt with SVD, exact derivatives, covariance, bounds) already exists in Rust and can be lifted, which makes the general fit a port rather than a project. Reached from the workbench (L009) as a native Node module (napi), a process over stdio (the command line itself, JSON in and out), or compiled to WebAssembly and loaded in the extension host.
  • TypeScript, in the extension host: no bridge at all, one language for the whole app; the fits written and tested anew, against SciPy; slower only where it does not matter for a practice's hundred points.
  • Python with compiled kernels, the difes habit: SciPy's curve_fit for free, the students' later language; the interpreter shipped inside the app, and a slower round trip per keystroke.

First opinion: Rust, reached as a process over stdio first (the command line is then the only interface, and the same for tests, scripts and the app) and as a native module when the Bench needs the latency; the fits tested against SciPy (I11) and a Python binding later for the students who go on.

decided (L013) Rust, over stdio first; a native Node module later if the Bench needs the latency; diff-ref-struct's least-squares engine lifted for the general fit.

Q2 · The app's name

lab-plot-fit is the working name (L005). The app's name is Josu's; a Basque name would suit the students, and the seed-place takes candidates.

decided (L013) lab-plot-fit kept for now; the real name when the app exists.

Q3 · How far KaleidaGraph's files are read

Its text files yes, from the first version: the library of definitions, the macro sources, the formula scripts, the text exports of data and fit results (I10). Its binary files (.qda, .qpc, .qpl, .eqn) only if a specification is found or the format is read from the examples with certainty; otherwise the app says what it cannot open and why.

decided (L013) Text files only; the binary formats are not read.

Q4 · The languages of the interface

APP_CONVENTIONS.md §10 says the text is English. The students work in Basque and Spanish. The first opinion: the app's chrome in English, Basque and Spanish from the start (the strings are a table, the cost is small when it is paid early), and the templates (I02) in the language the teacher writes them in; numbers shown with the system's decimal separator, files written with a point, Basque excepted where Josu's rule of the decimal comma applies.

decided (L010) English and Spanish for now, Basque later (R12). In the Code – OSS fork the chrome's strings are VS Code's own localisation bundles; the modules' strings are written as package.nls.json and package.nls.es.json from the first commit. The templates in the teacher's language, Spanish for the UNED course.

Q5 · The teacher and the student

Is a prepared practice (I02) a locked file the student fills, or an ordinary project the student may change? Is the class view (I06) wanted in the first year, or later? Should the expected values be hidden at the Bench until the series is complete?

decided (L013, L014) Prepared but editable: the student may add columns, plots and fits; the teacher's wiring is marked and protected. The template decides whether expected values are shown at the Bench, hidden by default. The class view (I06) stays in the seed-place for later; templates are R13.

Q6 · The first practice

Which practice of Técnicas Experimentales I is built first, as the reference the first version must serve end to end: its data sheet, its plots, its fits, its report. The core's tests and the tutorial film (I12) would follow it. The practices are now read (the eight, L011). Proposed: Ley de Hooke first, because real data of a session exist for both its series (datos_hooke_1.csv, datos_hooke_2.csv), it has two fits (P against Δx, T\(^{2}\) against M) and two derived quantities with the course's two error rules; Péndulo físico second, for the Bench's row of five repeats and its two plots; Descarga del condensador third, for a timed series and the exponential beside the linearised fit.

decided (L013) Ley de Hooke first, then Péndulo físico and Descarga del condensador.

On this page

    What this is

    The working site of one project: to build a Mac app with the features of KaleidaGraph and the window of the TRAE IDE, for the laboratory practices of the Physics degree, with a live bench mode where the student's readings become plots and fits as they are typed. It holds the brief, the study of KaleidaGraph, the design, the seed-place of ideas, a dated log of every step, the release notes and the manuals of the core and the app.
