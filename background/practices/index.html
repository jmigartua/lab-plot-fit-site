The practices

The practices: Técnicas Experimentales I

Requirements R04 and R11, log L011. The eight practices of the course as Josu teaches it, read from his guides: what each one measures and with what, the tables the student fills, the plots, the fits and what each gives with its error formula as the guide writes it, and what the Bench shows during the session. Generated from data/practices.yml; the guides it cites lie in practices/uned/tecI/, which is not published.

Técnicas Experimentales I · Grado en Física, UNED, taught at the Facultad de Ciencia y Tecnología, UPV/EHU (Leioa) · 2024–2025 · 8 practices, one report per practice, individual, handed in as one PDF.

The course's conventions

The course's error guide (errores.pdf, 4 pages): a result is always x \(\pm\) Δx with its unit; errors are given with one significant figure (one and a half exceptionally); the best value of n repeated readings is their mean and its error the "error cuadrático" Δx = √(Σ(x̄ − x_k)² / (n(n − 1))), used only when it exceeds the instrument's resolution, which is otherwise the error; an indirect quantity y = f(p, q, …) has Δy by quadrature, √(Σ (∂f/∂p · Δp)²), or by the upper bound Σ |∂f/∂p| Δp for quick estimates. The guides use both: Δg = |4π²/\(\mathrm{m}^{2}\)| Δm (upper bound, one variable) and Δk by quadrature (two variables). The regression guide (regresiones.pdf, 4 pages) sends the student to KaleidaGraph: data in the table, Formula Entry for a column operation (C2 = 2*C1 + C0), Gallery ▸ Linear ▸ Scatter, Curve Fit ▸ General ▸ fit1 (y = m1 + m2·M0) with Display Equation for the parameters and their errors, File ▸ Print Graphics, Save Graph and Save Data. The course site's own advice for the session: "introducid datos en el ordenador a medida que medís y verificad en tiempo real si el experimento está saliendo bien", which is the Bench of R05 in the course's words.

1 · Péndulo físico

P1 · The physical pendulum · 5 questions

The period T of a thin rod oscillating about axes at distances b from its centre of mass; from T(b), the gravitational acceleration g and the rod's radius of gyration k, three ways.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
b, axis to centre of mass the rod's notches every centimetre, a ruler m the ruler's resolution; b \(\ge 6\) cm
20 T, twenty oscillations chronometer s five repeats per b; the mean and its error
L, the rod's length ruler m resolution; for the theoretical k

Tables

  • T against b, 14 rows: b (m) measured; 20T₁ … 20T₅ (s) repeated \(\times 5\); mean of 20T (s) derived: mean of the five; T (s) derived: mean / 20; bT\(^{2}\) (m·\(\mathrm{s}^{2}\)) derived; b\(^{2}\) (\(\mathrm{m}^{2}\)) derived

Plots

  • T against b: the curve has a minimum at b = k; a high density of points near the minimum is asked for, to read k graphically
  • bT\(^{2}\) against b\(^{2}\): a straight line: slope 4π²/g, intercept 4π²k\(^{2}\)/g

Fits, and what they give

  • bT\(^{2}\) = m·b\(^{2}\) + n, least squares
    • g: g = 4π²/m; Δg = |4π²/\(\mathrm{m}^{2}\)| · |Δm|
    • k: k = √(n·g/4π²); Δk = (1/4π) · √((n/g)·Δg\(^{2}\) + (g/n)·Δn\(^{2}\))

Results table

k (graphical, from the minimum of T(b)), k (theory, L/(2√3)), k (regression), g (regression).

At the Bench

Each b is a row; the five chronometer readings arrive one by one and the row's mean, T, bT\(^{2}\) and b\(^{2}\) fill themselves; the T(b) curve shows the minimum forming, and the bT\(^{2}\) against b\(^{2}\) line with g and k and their errors updating. A reading of 20T far from its four siblings is marked.

2 · Ley de Hooke

P2 · Hooke's law · 5 questions

The spring constant K of a helical spring by a static method (loads and elongations) and a dynamic one (periods of oscillation against the mass), the effective mass αm of the spring, and the spring's mass three ways.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
M, the load balance; calibrated masses g → kg the balance's resolution
Δx, the elongation vertical scale in millimetres m resolution
t₂₀, twenty oscillations chronometer s resolution and reaction; one reading per mass in the guide
the spring's dimensions micrometer m resolution; for the volume

Tables

  • Static: load against elongation, 10 rows: M (g) measured; P = M·g (N) derived; Δx (m) measured
  • Dynamic: period against mass, 12 rows: M (g) measured; M (kg) derived: unit change; t₂₀ (s) measured; T (s) derived: t₂₀/20; T\(^{2}\) (\(\mathrm{s}^{2}\)) derived

Plots

  • P against Δx: a straight line through the origin whose slope is K
  • T\(^{2}\) against M: a straight line: slope 4π²/K, intercept 4π²αm/K

Fits, and what they give

  • P = K·Δx (+ n), least squares
    • K (static): the slope; ΔK = \(\Delta\)(slope)
  • T\(^{2}\) = a·M + b, least squares
    • K (dynamic): K = 4π²/a; ΔK = |4π²/a\(^{2}\)| · |Δa|
    • αm: αm = b/a; \(\Delta\)(αm) = (1/a\(^{2}\)) · √(a\(^{2}\)·Δb\(^{2}\) + b\(^{2}\)·Δa\(^{2}\))
    • m (spring), from αm with α = 1/3: m = 3·αm; 3·\(\Delta\)(αm)

Results table

K (static), K (dynamic), αm, m spring (from αm), m spring (balance), m spring (volume × density, ρ = 8.0 \(\pm\) 0.2 g/\(\mathrm{cm}^{3}\)).

At the Bench

Two series in one session; each load's elongation puts a point on the P–Δx line and K updates; each t₂₀ puts a point on the T\(^{2}\)–M line and K, αm update; the two K's are shown side by side as they converge or not. Real data of a session exist (datos_hooke_1.csv, datos_hooke_2.csv) and are the first test data of the core.

3 · Plano inclinado

P3 · The inclined plane · 5 questions

A spring's constant from the period of a cart oscillating on an inclined track; two springs in series and in parallel; then g from the cart's acceleration down the track at seven inclinations, with and without extra mass.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
M, the cart's mass balance g the balance's resolution
t, three oscillations chronometer s five repeats; mean and its error
h, the track's height; its length tape m resolution; sin \(\theta\) derived
\(x_0\), x_f, the cart's positions the track's scale m resolution; d = x_f − \(x_0\)
t, the run down the track chronometer s four repeats; mean and \(\sigma\)_t

Tables

  • Periods: one spring, two in series, two in parallel, 3 rows: t₁ … t₅ (s) repeated \(\times 5\); T mean (s) derived: mean/3 (three cycles timed); ΔT mean (s) derived: the error of the mean
  • Runs at seven inclinations (twice: without and with extra mass), 7 rows: h (cm) measured: 10, 9, … 4; sin \(\theta\) derived: h / track length; \(x_0\), x_f, d (m) measured, derived; t₁ … t₄ (s) repeated \(\times 4\); t̄, \(\sigma\)_t (s) derived; a = 2d/t̄² (m/\(\mathrm{s}^{2}\)) derived; no error asked, significant figures by eye

Plots

  • a against sin \(\theta\): a straight line whose slope is g; twice, without and with extra mass

Fits, and what they give

  • a = g·sin \(\theta\) (+ n), least squares
    • g (without load): the slope; \(\Delta\)(slope)
    • g (with load): the slope; \(\Delta\)(slope)
  • no fit: k from T = 2π√(m/k)
    • k, k_eff series, k_eff parallel: k = 4π² m / T\(^{2}\); propagated from Δm and ΔT (question 1)

Results table

M cart, k (one spring), k_eff series, k_eff parallel, g (without load), g (with load).

At the Bench

The repeated timings of one row are the unit of entry: five (or four) readings, the mean and its error at once, the row's k or a; the a–sin \(\theta\) line grows one row per inclination with g updating; a timing far from its siblings is marked (the reaction-time question is visible in the data).

4 · Corriente continua I

P4 · Direct current I · 4 questions

Resistances in series and in parallel measured and computed; the electromotive force and internal resistance of a dry cell and of a generator-plus-potentiometer source, from the voltage across several load resistances.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
\(R_1\), \(R_2\), R₃ and their series and parallel combinations multimeter as ohmmeter \(\Omega\) the multimeter's resolution and accuracy
V across the source with R = 220, 100, 50 \(\Omega\) multimeter as voltmeter V resolution and accuracy; read quickly (heating)

Tables

  • Resistances, 7 rows: R measured (\(\Omega\)) measured; R series, parallel (theory) (\(\Omega\)) derived: ΣR; (Σ1/R)⁻¹
  • Source under load (twice: the cell, the generator + potentiometer), 3 rows: R (\(\Omega\)) measured; V (V) measured; 1/R, 1/V (\(\Omega\)⁻¹, V\(^{-1}\)) derived

Plots

  • 1/V against 1/R: a straight line: intercept 1/\(\varepsilon\), slope r_i/\(\varepsilon\)

Fits, and what they give

  • 1/V = (1/\(\varepsilon\)) + (r_i/\(\varepsilon\))·(1/R), least squares
    • \(\varepsilon\): \(\varepsilon\) = 1/intercept; propagated from \(\Delta\)(intercept)
    • r_i: r_i = slope/intercept; propagated from both (quadrature)

Results table

R series and parallel, measured and theoretical, \(\varepsilon\) and r_i of the cell, \(\varepsilon\) and r_i of the effective source, \(\varepsilon\) measured directly.

At the Bench

Few points (three loads per source) and the fit is in reciprocals: the Bench shows 1/V against 1/R with the line and \(\varepsilon\), r_i, and warns that with three points the errors mean little; the power dissipated in R is computed before the reading (the guide's warning) and shown.

5 · Corriente continua II

P5 · Direct current II · 4 questions

The effect of the instruments on the circuit: three resistances measured with the voltmeter and ammeter in the short and the long arrangement; voltage drops across resistances in series at three scales; the characteristic curve I(V) of a filament lamp.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
V and I for R \(\approx 50\) \(\Omega\), 500 \(\Omega\), 1 MΩ, in two arrangements two multimeters V, A resolution and accuracy; the 10 A input never used
V₁, V₂, V₃ across two resistances in series, three scales one voltmeter V resolution and accuracy
V and I of the lamp, \(\ge 20\) voltages, half of them in 0–500 mV two multimeters, a 100 \(\Omega\) series resistor mV, mA resolution and accuracy

Tables

  • Short and long arrangement, 6 rows: V, I (V, A) measured; R = V/I (\(\Omega\)) derived; R ohmmeter (\(\Omega\)) measured
  • Drops in series, 3 rows: V₁, V₂, V₃ (V) measured; V₁ + V₂ (V) derived; compared with V₃
  • The lamp's characteristic, 20 rows: V (mV) measured; I (mA) measured; V/I (\(\Omega\)) derived: the resistance at each point

Plots

  • I against V: not a straight line: the filament's resistance rises with temperature; the slope at the origin is 1/R cold
  • V/I against V: the resistance against the voltage, to read between which values it varied (proposal: the guide asks the question, not the plot)

Fits, and what they give

  • none required; proposal: a straight line through the first points (0–500 mV) for R cold, and R = V/I per point
    • R cold: the initial slope; \(\Delta\)(slope)

Results table

R by the short and long arrangements against the ohmmeter, V₁ + V₂ against V₃ at three scales, the lamp's resistance, cold and hot.

At the Bench

The lamp's curve is the Bench at its most useful: twenty points typed as the voltage is raised, the curve bending as the filament heats, the point density in 0–500 mV checked against the guide's requirement (half the points there) and said so.

6 · Descarga del condensador

P6 · Discharge of a capacitor · 3 questions

The time constant RC of an RC circuit from the half-life t½ measured repeatedly, and from the discharge curve V(t) linearised as ln V against t; the same without the external resistance (the voltmeter as R); comparison with the nominal R·C.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
R nominal (colour code) and measured multimeter as ohmmeter \(\Omega\) the code's tolerance; the multimeter's
t½, the time for V to halve chronometer, voltmeter s five repeats; mean and its error
V at instants t of one discharge (15–20 points), intervals > 1 s voltmeter with hold, chronometer V, s resolution; the meter's 1 s response

Tables

  • Resistances by colour code, 3 rows: R nominal, R measured (\(\Omega\)) measured
  • Half-life, 5 rows: t½ (s) repeated \(\times 5\); mean t½ \(\pm\) error; RC = t½/ln 2 (s) derived
  • Discharge V(t), with R and without, 15 rows: t (s) measured; V (V) measured; ln V derived

Plots

  • ln V against t: a straight line: slope −1/RC, intercept ln V₀; the fitted line drawn on the same figure
  • V against t: the exponential itself (proposal: the app fits V = V₀ e^(−t/RC) directly as well, and compares with the linearised fit)

Fits, and what they give

  • ln V = m·t + b, least squares
    • RC: RC = −1/m; \(\Delta\)(RC) = |Δm/\(\mathrm{m}^{2}\)| (question 3)
    • V₀: V₀ = e^b; V₀·Δb
  • proposal: V = V₀·exp(−t/\(\tau\)), nonlinear least squares, weighted
    • \(\tau\) = RC: the fit's parameter; from the covariance; compared with the linearised value

Results table

RC from t½, RC from the ln V–t slope, with R, RC without external R (the voltmeter's resistance), RC nominal (R measured × C nominal), the discrepancy explained.

At the Bench

The discharge is timed: readings arrive every few seconds while the capacitor empties, and the Bench must take them without the student looking away; the ln V–t line and RC update per reading, and the departure from the line at low V (the meter's response, the electrolytic capacitor) becomes visible as it happens.

7 · Ondas estacionarias

P7 · Standing waves on a string · 6 questions

The resonance frequencies of a string with fixed ends against the mode number n at constant tension, and against the tension at fixed n; the wave speed and the string's linear density; an elastic and an inelastic string.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
l, the string's length tape m resolution; remeasured per tension for the elastic string
m, the hanging mass; T = m·g balance; masses kg → N the balance's resolution
f_n, the resonance of mode n function generator and mechanical vibrator Hz the generator's resolution; the sharpness of the resonance

Tables

  • f_n against n at constant T (one table per tension, ≥ 3 tensions), 5 rows: n constant: 1 … 5; f_n (Hz) measured; λ_n = 2l/n (m) derived; v = f_n·λ_n (m/s) derived
  • f_n against T at fixed n, 5 rows: m, T = m·g, √T (kg, N, √N) measured, derived; l (m) measured (elastic string); f_n (Hz) measured

Plots

  • f_n against n: a straight line through the origin: slope v/(2l); from it v and, with T, μ
  • f_n against √T: a straight line: slope n/(2l√μ); from it μ; for the elastic string μ changes with T and the line bends

Fits, and what they give

  • f_n = (v/2l)·n (+ b), least squares
    • v: v = 2l·slope; propagated from \(\Delta\)(slope) and Δl
    • μ: μ = T/v\(^{2}\); propagated
  • f_n = s·√T (+ b), least squares
    • μ: μ = (n/(2l·s))²; propagated

Results table

v and μ from f against n, per tension, μ from f against √T, v measured against v = √(T/μ).

At the Bench

The resonance is found by sweeping the generator: the student types f_n when the amplitude peaks; the f–n line and v, μ update, and the intercept's departure from zero is shown. Real data of both strings exist (datos_cuerda_elastica.csv, datos_cuerda_rigida.csv): test data for the core, with the elastic string's non-linearity in them.

8 · Vaporización del agua

P8 · The latent heat of vaporisation of water · 6 questions

The heat of vaporisation q of water from the mass of water condensed in a fixed time \(\tau\) at an electrical power W = I·V: by the method of differences with two powers, and by a straight line of M against E = W·\(\tau\) with ten powers.

Measured

What Instrument Unit Uncertainty
I and V of the heater ammeter and voltmeter; a rheostat A, V resolution and accuracy
\(\tau\), the collection time chronometer s resolution; 360 s suggested
the mass of the empty and the full beaker balance g the balance's resolution

Tables

  • Two powers (differences), 2 rows: I, V (A, V) measured; W = I·V (W) derived; beaker empty, beaker + water, M (g) measured, derived
  • Ten powers (regression), 10 rows: I, V, W (A, V, W) measured, derived; E = W·\(\tau\) (J, cal) derived: unit change 1 cal = 4.186 J; M (g) measured (by difference)

Plots

  • M against E: a straight line: slope 1/q; the intercept ideally zero (questions 4 and 5); E on the abscissa because it is what was set

Fits, and what they give

  • M = s·E + n, least squares
    • q: q = 1/s; Δq = |Δs/\(\mathrm{s}^{2}\)|
    • n: the intercept; Δn; its meaning asked
  • no fit: differences
    • q: q = (W₁ − W₂)·\(\tau\) / (M₁ − M₂); propagated

Results table

q by differences, q by regression, with the discrepancy from 530 cal/g.

At the Bench

One row per power, six minutes each: the session is long and the Bench quiet; each row's W, E and M fill themselves from I, V and the two weighings, the M–E line and q update, and the running comparison with 530 cal/g is shown or hidden at the teacher's choice.

What the app must do, read off the eight

  • Tables with repeated readings in a row. Five chronometer readings of one b (P1), five timings of one spring (P3), five half-lives (P6): a row holds repeats, and their mean and the error of the mean (the course's "error cuadrático") are columns the app fills. No practice needs a table wider than twelve columns.
  • Derived columns by formula, with units. T = t₂₀/20, bT\(^{2}\), b\(^{2}\), P = M·g, sin \(\theta\) = h/L, a = 2d/t\(^{2}\), ln V, λ = 2l/n, E = W·\(\tau\) in joules and calories: every practice has them, and two of them change units on the way (g → kg, J → cal). Units on columns (I03) are not a luxury here; they are what the guides write in every header.
  • Nine straight-line fits, and nothing else is required. Every fit the guides ask for is y = m·x + n by least squares, with m and n and their errors Δm and Δn: bT\(^{2}\) against b\(^{2}\) (P1), P against Δx and T\(^{2}\) against M (P2), a against sin \(\theta\) (P3), 1/V against 1/R (P4), ln V against t (P6), f against n and f against √T (P7), M against E (P8). P5's lamp curve is plotted, not fitted. The general nonlinear fit (KaleidaGraph's, and the proposal for P6's exponential) is a second step, not the first.
  • Physical quantities from the fit's parameters, with propagated errors. g = 4π²/m, K = 4π²/a, αm = b/a, RC = −1/m, q = 1/s, \(\varepsilon\) = 1/n, r_i = m/n: a derived quantity is a formula on the parameters, with its error by the course's two rules (quadrature; the upper bound). The results table of each practice lists them; the app's fit report must give them directly (I03, I11).
  • The course's conventions, exactly. Errors with one significant figure; the mean and its error for repeats, the instrument's resolution when it is larger; propagation by quadrature or by the upper bound as the guide chooses. The app states which rule it applies (Settings ▸ Fits) and defaults to the guide's.
  • KaleidaGraph's regression, reproduced. The course's regression guide sends students to KaleidaGraph's Curve Fit ▸ General ▸ fit1 (y = m1 + m2·M0) with Display Equation: its parameters and their errors are what the students have been handing in. The app's linear fit must give the same numbers on the same data (I11), and the fit's label on the plot the same information.
  • The Bench is the course's own instruction. The course site tells the student: "introducid datos en el ordenador a medida que medís y verificad en tiempo real si el experimento está saliendo bien". Every practice has a natural unit of live entry (a row of repeats, a point of a curve), and three are timed (P6's discharge, P5's lamp, P8's six-minute rows).
  • Real data to test on. Two practices ship data of real sessions (P2 Hooke, two tables; P7 the two strings, with the elastic string's non-linearity): the core's first tests, and the first Bench replays (I07).
On this page

    What this is

    The working site of one project: to build a Mac app with the features of KaleidaGraph and the window of the TRAE IDE, for the laboratory practices of the Physics degree, with a live bench mode where the student's readings become plots and fits as they are typed. It holds the brief, the study of KaleidaGraph, the design, the seed-place of ideas, a dated log of every step, the release notes and the manuals of the core and the app.
