Release notes

Release notes

What each version of the core and the app adds, changes and fixes, and what it still does not do; newest first, current version 0.6.0. The same text is written to core/CHANGELOG.md. Generated from data/releases.yml; do not edit by hand.

0.6.02026-10-17release

Measurement uncertainty by the GUM, and Monte Carlo by its Supplement 1

R18 (L022): the GUM uncertainty framework with an uncertainty budget, effective degrees of freedom, the coverage factor and the expanded uncertainty; the propagation of distributions by Monte Carlo with coverage intervals; the validation of the framework against the Monte Carlo result; the course's error rules mapped onto the GUM's terms. Checked against closed forms.

Added

  • gum: inputs with a distribution: Gaussian (u, optional degrees of freedom), Type A from the readings (the mean, s/√n, n − 1), rectangular (a/√3), the resolution of an instrument (δ/√12), triangular (a/√6). framework: the sensitivity coefficients, each contribution and its share of u_c\(^{2}\), u_c, ν_eff by Welch–Satterthwaite, k from Student's t at the coverage probability, U = k·u_c and the coverage interval. monte_carlo: M trials with a seed (xoshiro256**, deterministic), the estimate, u, the probabilistically symmetric and the shortest coverage intervals, the skewness; a Type A input sampled as a scaled t. validate: Supplement 1's tolerance from u written to one or two significant digits.
  • rng: xoshiro256** with uniform, Gaussian, gamma, chi-square and t draws; no dependency.
  • lpf gum "<model>" --input a=2.369:gauss:0.077:11 --input d=1.5:res:0.01 --input t=typeA:7.40,7.42,… [--p 0.95] [--mc 200000] [--seed 1] [--digits 1]: the framework, the Monte Carlo and the validation in one report.
  • Test gum: the Type B standard uncertainties; Type A equal to the course's error cuadrático; k(95 %, ν = 4) = 2.776 and 1.960 for infinite ν; a sum with Welch–Satterthwaite and a product with the relative law, equal to the course's quadrature; the Monte Carlo of a sum within 1 % of u_c and validated; x\(^{2}\) with x ~ N(0, 1), where the law of propagation gives zero and the Monte Carlo √2 and is not validated; the resolution sampled rectangular; a Type A input as a scaled t; determinism by seed.

Known limitations

  • No correlated inputs yet (the covariance terms of GUM 5.2); no adaptive Monte Carlo (Supplement 1, 7.9: M is fixed, 200 000 by default); the project's derived quantities use the course's rules, with the GUM report by command for now.
0.5.02026-10-17release

KaleidaGraph's Exponential, Logarithmic and Power fits, the Linear fit's statistics, confidence bands

The manual's curve-fit chapter read (pp. 151–154, 173–175, 180; L021): the three standard fits are least squares on linearised data and show no errors, the General fit is Levenberg–Marquardt with standard errors, the Linear fit lists adjusted R\(^{2}\), standard errors, t, F, P values and 95 % confidence limits, and confidence bands can be copied to the data window. All of it in the core, checked against SciPy.

Added

  • presets::fit_preset: Exponential y = m0·e^(m1·x), Logarithmic y = m0 + m1·log(x) (base 10, believed), Power y = m0·x^m1, by linearisation as KaleidaGraph's standard fits (the method named, the errors propagated from the line and marked as such) or as the General fit of the same equation (--method nonlinear, started from the linearised solution); R between the fitted curve and the data; the manual's data restrictions refused with its words.
  • linear::statistics: adjusted R\(^{2}\), the standard error of the estimate, t and P values of m and n, F and its P value, the confidence limits at the level asked for; none through the origin, as KaleidaGraph. linear::band: the confidence band of the line and the prediction band at any x.
  • dist: Student's t and Fisher's F through the regularised incomplete beta function; t quantiles.
  • The project's model fits restart from their last solution when there is one (a warm start for the Bench).
  • lpf fit … --preset exponential|logarithmic|power [--method nonlinear], lpf fit … --stats [--level 0.95], lpf fit … --band data|x1,x2,… [--prediction].
  • Test presets: the three presets both ways against NumPy's polyfit on the transformed data and SciPy's curve_fit; the statistics against linregress and the t and F distributions to 1e-6 or better; the bands at three x; the distributions themselves.

Known limitations

  • The base of the Logarithmic fit's log and the convention of the General fit's standard errors are read from the manual, to be confirmed on the demo with the Hooke data (I11).
0.4.02026-10-17release

The project and its dependency graph: a cell changes, what depends on it follows

R15 in the core (L020): a project of tables, formulas, fits and derived quantities as nodes of a graph; a change to a cell recomputes what depends on it, in order, once, and returns what changed, which is the event the Bench shows (R05). The project is a JSON file with its results, read and written by the command line and, later, by the app. Checked on Hooke's practice as a whole project.

Added

  • project: Project with named tables, FormulaSpec (a formula on a table), FitSpec (a line, a polynomial or a model on two columns, weights from a column or auto from the y column's uncertainties), DerivedSpec (an expression over the fits' parameters, fitname_param, and other derived quantities, its error propagated by quadrature or the upper bound); the results kept with the project (fit_results, derived_results).
  • Project::graph: the nodes (data columns, formula columns, fits, derived) with their dependencies read from the expressions; a missing dependency is named; a cycle is refused. downstream: the nodes after a change, in evaluation order. run: everything, in order. set_cell: a cell set or cleared (a row past the end extends the table), then only the downstream recomputed, each node's outcome reported (a failing node reports its message and the rest go on).
  • lpf project <file.json> graph | run | set table.column[row]=value … [--save].
  • Test project: Hooke's practice as a project (both series, T and T\(^{2}\), the two line fits, K by the guide's upper bound, αm by quadrature, the spring's mass); a reading corrected at the Bench recomputes T, T\(^{2}\), the dynamic fit and its three derived quantities and nothing of the static series, and equals a fresh run; a row added past the end; the JSON round trip; a model fit with auto weights and a derived RC; a formula that fails (ln of a voltage) reports and the rest runs; a cycle refused.

Known limitations

  • Fits name their parameters m, n / c0… / the model's names; a derived quantity cannot yet use a column's statistic (the mean of a whole column); no plots in the project yet (phase 2's shared component reads the fits and tables); KaleidaGraph's standard fit presets, confidence bands and the fit1 comparison still to do.
0.3.02026-10-17release

Formulas on columns, with units and propagated uncertainties

R14 in the core (L018): a formula name[unit] = expr over the table's columns, evaluated in SI, its unit derived and checked (metres plus seconds refused), written in the unit asked for; the uncertainty of every cell propagated from the columns' rules by quadrature or by the upper bound; repeats in a row by mean and sem; the constant g0. Checked on Hooke's series against the guides' closed forms.

Added

  • units: a unit as a factor and a vector of dimensions; the SI base and derived units (N, J, W, Pa, V, \(\Omega\), C, F, Hz, T, H), cal, eV, L, min, h, deg, %, with prefixes; products, quotients and powers as the guides write them (m/s^2, N/m, kg·m/s^2); conversion between units of one dimension; the SI name of any dimension.
  • expr: functions of several values in a row, mean, sem, sd, min, max, sum; the unit of an expression from its variables' units, with the rules (a sum needs equal dimensions, an exponent of a quantity must be a plain number, a function wants no unit, sqrt halves the dimensions).
  • formula: name[unit] = expr parsed and applied; columns by name or by cN; values in SI, result converted; uncertainties propagated cell by cell with numerical partial derivatives, kept with the column (Uncertainty::Values); empty cells give empty results.
  • lpf formula <file.csv> "T = t20/20" "T2[s^2] = T^2" … [--err col=value,…] [--upper] [--out file.csv].
  • Test formulas: the units' parsing and conversions; Hooke's columns as the guide asks (g → kg, T = t20/20, T\(^{2}\) with ΔT\(^{2}\) = 2TΔT, P = M·g0 in N, the refusals); repeats in a row (Δmean = \(\Delta\)/√5 by quadrature, \(\Delta\) by the upper bound), sem equal to the course's error cuadrático, \(\Delta\)(b/a) against the guide's closed form.

Known limitations

  • Column-wise aggregates (the mean of a whole column) are not formulas yet (they are lpf stats); no dependency graph (R15); KaleidaGraph's standard fit presets, confidence bands and the fit1 comparison still to do.
0.2.02026-10-17release

The general curve fit, the polynomial fit, and an expression grammar

KaleidaGraph's General and Polynomial fits in the core (L017): any equation y = f(x; p) fitted by Gauss–Newton with Marquardt damping on the scaled normal matrix solved by eigen-decomposition, the method of diff-ref-struct's engine written for small problems; bounds, singular directions named, the errors in the same three conventions as the line; polynomials to order 9; the equations compiled once by a grammar of their own. Checked against SciPy's curve_fit and NumPy.

Added

  • expr: a compiled arithmetic grammar (+ − * / ^, unary minus below ^, 14 functions, pi and e, named variables) for equations and formulas; KaleidaGraph's definitions (a + b*x, a*exp(-x/b)) compile as they are.
  • nonlinear::fit_model: the general fit of an equation in x with named parameters and starting values; the Jacobian by central differences; damping only when a step would raise \(\chi^2\); bounds held with the gradient pushing out; convergence on the computed shift; errors from the scatter, from the weights, or scaled; correlations, R\(^{2}\), the stop reason as data, the parameters the data cannot fix named.
  • solve: the scaled normal matrix, Jacobi eigen-decomposition, singular directions below \(10^{-12}\) of the largest eigenvalue, the damped step and the inverse.
  • poly::fit_polynomial: y = c₀ + … + c_k x^k to order 9 by the same solve on a centred, scaled design matrix, the covariance mapped back to the x coefficients.
  • lpf fit … --model "<equation>" --start a=1,b=2 [--bounds a=0:inf], lpf fit … --poly <order>, lpf eval "<expr>" --with a=1.
  • Test fits: Hooke's dynamic series as a nonlinear fit (equal to the closed-form line to 1e-9), a synthetic capacitor discharge V₀·exp(−t/\(\tau\)) against curve_fit unweighted, weighted in both conventions and bounded (\(\tau\) held at 10), a polynomial of order 2 against NumPy with its covariance, the grammar's precedence, and the refusals (a singular pair, a missing parameter, too few points).

Known limitations

  • KaleidaGraph's standard Exponential, Logarithmic and Power fits are presets to write (their method, linearised or not, is to be read in the manual's pages 153–154 first); no confidence bands yet; no formulas on columns with units; no dependency graph; KaleidaGraph's fit1 numbers not yet compared.
0.1.02026-10-17release

The Rust core and Hooke's two fits

The first build of the core (L016): the crate lpf with its command line, tables with units and uncertainty rules, repeated readings by the course's rule, the straight-line fit with its errors in three conventions, and the propagation of errors; the first practice's two series checked against SciPy.

Added

  • The lpf crate in core/: a library for the app and the lpf command line, JSON out (lpf read, lpf stats, lpf fit, lpf propagate, lpf --version).
  • The table: columns with name, unit, values and an uncertainty rule (none, constant, relative, a column); CSV with name(unit) headers read and written, the delimiter found, a decimal comma accepted; KaleidaGraph's c0, c1 … as column addresses.
  • Repeated readings: the mean, the course's "error cuadrático", the sample deviation, the error taken against the instrument's resolution; a result written with one significant figure in the error.
  • The straight line y = m·x + n: unweighted (errors from the scatter, SciPy's convention), through the origin, weighted by \(\sigma\) (errors from the weights, or scaled by √(\(\chi^2\)/dof)); r, R\(^{2}\), residuals, the m–n correlation.
  • Propagation by quadrature and by the upper bound, with each error's contribution; lpf propagate takes an expression.
  • Test hooke: both series of the real Hooke data against NumPy and SciPy (tests/data/hooke_reference.py), K, ΔK, αm, \(\Delta\)(αm) by the guide's own formulas.

Known limitations

  • No general nonlinear fit yet (diff-ref-struct's engine to be lifted); no formulas with units; no dependency graph; KaleidaGraph's fit1 numbers not yet compared.

    What this is

    The working site of one project: to build a Mac app with the features of KaleidaGraph and the window of the TRAE IDE, for the laboratory practices of the Physics degree, with a live bench mode where the student's readings become plots and fits as they are typed. It holds the brief, the study of KaleidaGraph, the design, the seed-place of ideas, a dated log of every step, the release notes and the manuals of the core and the app.
